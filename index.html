Solar follows the wind blowing from the Sun right now, measured by a spacecraft at L1, a million miles sunward of Earth.

Its speed moves the smoke, its density thickens it, and its pressure sizes the magnetic bubble that turns it aside. When its field points south, some of it slips in at the poles and the auroras brighten.

The Earth shows the real season and starts facing the Sun as it does now, then turns faster than life. Distances are not to scale.
